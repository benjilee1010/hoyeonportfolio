Website redesign · Nonprofit

TerpTax

A redesign of the website for a nonprofit that offers free tax preparation to eligible students and community members in College Park, Maryland.

Role
Everything on the website itself: design, layout, copy and code
Worked with
A friend, who handled the professor and the backend setup
Site
terptax.pages.dev
1

Starting point

An early version, not yet designed around its visitors

I was handed an early version of the site that hadn't been through a UX pass, and I took over everything about the website itself. My friend handled communication with the professor who gave us the work, along with the backend setup: Cloudflare and the email domain.

The page wasn't very user friendly or pleasant to look at, and it was inconsistent and disorganized. The flow was the biggest problem. It led with how to book and what to do, and only afterward explained what TerpTax can help with.

2

Design decisions

Make the site reflect what TerpTax is

I put a lot of weight on a site's design reflecting the message of the organization behind it. TerpTax is a nonprofit that helps people through something stressful, and tax filing is also a clinical task. The design had to feel both.

  • Order. The home page now opens with "What brings you here?", then covers what TerpTax can help with, how getting help works, and what to bring.
  • Buttons. My first redesign used fully rounded pill buttons, which felt too bubbly for tax filing. I changed them to a small corner radius: exact enough for a clinical task, soft enough to feel friendly.
  • Logo. The terrapin shell logo took several rounds: where it sits next to the name, its size, and how the T is centered inside the shell. I also tried a faint hex pattern in the background at a few opacities, then removed it.
  • Plain language. A later pass made the site easier for older and less technical visitors, with plainer copy and fewer promises made on TerpTax's behalf.
3

How it was built

A small site with a few moving parts

The redesign began as a single HTML page and is hosted on Cloudflare Pages. Booking goes through Calendly, for a consultation with the supervising CPA. I replaced the email links with a contact form that keeps the address out of the page and cuts spam.

My friend built the intake system behind the form. I redesigned its private reviewer pages to match the public site.
