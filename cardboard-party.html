Why I built it

It started as a different game

I'm a big fan of Marvel Rivals, and I wanted to make something similar: a hero arena game that was more of a party game, with simple visuals so it could run well on almost any system.

Partway through, balancing the characters turned out to be hard. I dropped it and went back to an idea from high school: a party kart racer that works on phones and computers, plays online, and is free. I wanted it to look consistent and feel well made.

2

Design decisions

Most of the work went into how it plays

The game is gameplay-first, so I spent most of my time on the experience of playing it rather than on the screens around it. That meant the timing of the drift, how the karts handle, the visual and sound effects, and how clearly the gameplay and the interface read.

The menus stay simple on purpose. The goal was pick up and play, so I avoided complex, detailed screens. I was aiming for more polish than projects like this usually have.

3

How it was built

A browser game with a server for online races

The game runs in the browser with three.js, and a Node server using WebSockets handles online races. There is also a desktop build made with Electron, and the live version is hosted on Render.

Karts charge drifts through blue, purple and rainbow stages, and races have items and bots. Tracks come from a drawn track editor, and there are themed maps including forest, cavern, candy land and space. Players meet in a party lobby and ready up before a race.
